When we think about video games, it’s easy to picture a console sitting in the living room and a gamepad on the couch. Four months into XBOX, I keep coming back to that image when I talk to folks. There’s so much more depth to games than you can see from the couch.

We work in entertainment. A game can grow into a world with its own art, music, and stories. Some of those worlds give birth to movies. Sometimes it all comes together so well that folks are still playing decades later. Bringing those worlds to life comes with some fascinating technical challenges.

We think a lot about the tools creators use to prototype ideas, test builds across devices, and distribute their games. That includes how they gather player feedback and use it in the next build. On the player side, we look at how people find games, join their friends, and make things together in the worlds they already love.

It’s getting harder to draw a line between what we can build today and what still feels a few years (months?) away. The tools are changing fast enough that the line can move in a week. Meanwhile, I’m trying to roll an Undead in WoW Forever so I can play with friends in Europe, and I’m still excited to go back to Hyrule in Ocarina of Time.

There’s a lot we’re still figuring out at XBOX, and getting to build things to see what feels right has been a fun part of these first few months. If you’re a designer or an engineer who likes working that way, I think you’d thrive here.
